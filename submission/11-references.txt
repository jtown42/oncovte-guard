WORDING NOTES (rationale for phrasing, in case a reviewer asks):

- "a leading cause of death": cancer-associated VTE is commonly described as the
  SECOND leading cause of death in patients with cancer (after cancer progression
  itself), supported by ITAC [1]. The submission uses the slightly more
  conservative "a leading cause," which is equally defensible.

- AVERT/CASSINI framing: the text credits the trials "and their pooled analysis,"
  not each trial's primary result. This is deliberate. AVERT showed a significant
  VTE reduction (4.2% vs 10.2%, HR 0.41, 95% CI 0.26-0.65) but with more major
  bleeding (3.5% vs 1.8%, HR 2.00, 95% CI 1.01-3.95). CASSINI's PRIMARY endpoint
  over the full 180-day period was NOT statistically significant (HR 0.66, 95% CI
  0.40-1.09, p=0.10); its benefit was significant only during the on-treatment
  window (HR 0.40, 95% CI 0.20-0.80). A pooled analysis of both trials shows a
  significant reduction in VTE (RR ~0.56, 95% CI 0.35-0.89) without a significant
  increase in major bleeding [1]. Citing the pooled result avoids overstating
  CASSINI's headline finding.

- Khorana threshold: the app applies the NCCN prophylaxis threshold of Khorana
  score >=2 (intermediate-to-high risk), which aligns with the AVERT/CASSINI
  inclusion criteria. Note this differs from the ORIGINAL Khorana high-risk cut
  point of >=3; the app is explicit about using >=2. NCCN population exclusions
  (multiple myeloma, acute leukemia, myeloproliferative neoplasms, primary/
  metastatic brain tumors) are handled on a separate "excluded" pathway, not
  scored against Khorana.
