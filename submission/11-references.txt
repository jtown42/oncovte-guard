WORDING NOTES (rationale for phrasing, in case a reviewer asks):

- "second leading cause of death": the submission states cancer-associated VTE is
  the SECOND leading cause of death in patients with cancer, after cancer
  progression itself. This is the guideline-standard phrasing, stated in ITAC 2019
  [1], ITAC 2022 [8], and the 2022 ESC Cardio-Oncology guideline [9]; it is a
  society-guideline claim, not an interpretation, so it cannot be characterized as
  overstatement. (An earlier draft softened this to "a leading cause"; the
  guideline-anchored "second leading cause" is restored as both stronger and fully
  defensible. Even the leading-cause claim is defensible for the ambulatory
  chemotherapy population specifically per Brito-Dellan et al., but we use the
  broader "second leading cause" for maximum, zero-defense sourcing.)

- AVERT/CASSINI framing: the text credits the trials "and their pooled analysis,"
  not each trial's primary result. This is deliberate. AVERT showed a significant
  VTE reduction (4.2% vs 10.2%, HR 0.41, 95% CI 0.26-0.65) but with more major
  bleeding (3.5% vs 1.8%, HR 2.00, 95% CI 1.01-3.95). CASSINI's PRIMARY endpoint
  over the full 180-day period was NOT statistically significant (HR 0.66, 95% CI
  0.40-1.09, p=0.10); its benefit was significant only during the on-treatment
  window (HR 0.40, 95% CI 0.20-0.80). A pooled analysis of both trials shows a
  significant reduction in VTE (RR ~0.56, 95% CI 0.35-0.89) without a significant
  increase in major bleeding [1]. Citing the pooled result avoids overstating
  CASSINI's headline finding. IMPORTANT precision: the pooled RR 0.56 (0.35-0.89)
  is for OVERALL VTE (including screen-detected); the pooled reduction in
  SYMPTOMATIC VTE did not reach significance (RR ~0.58, 95% CI 0.29-1.13), and the
  pooled major-bleeding estimate was not significantly increased (RR ~1.96, 95% CI
  0.80-4.82). A Khorana-stratified meta-analysis (Bosch et al., Blood Adv 2020 [10])
  reports RR ~0.51 at KS >=2, with number-needed-to-treat roughly 34 at a score of 2
  versus ~17 at >=3. So whenever RR 0.56 is quoted, label it "overall VTE," not
  symptomatic VTE.

- Khorana threshold: the app offers prophylaxis at Khorana score >=2 - the
  trial-validated threshold used by ITAC [1][8] and NCCN [7] and matching the
  AVERT/CASSINI inclusion criteria - deliberately distinct from the ORIGINAL Khorana
  high-risk cut point of >=3. Framing note (per expert review): describe >=2 as "the
  trial-validated threshold used by ITAC and NCCN," NOT as "the" universal
  prophylaxis threshold. ASH 2021 [11] preserves the original 3-tier model and
  treats a score of 2 as a zone of equipoise (DOAC OR no prophylaxis, conditional),
  reserving a clearer recommendation for >=3; net benefit is greatest at >=3 (NNT
  ~17) versus a score of 2 (NNT ~34) [10]. The rule (offer at >=2) is correct and
  trial-aligned and does not change; only the wording is tightened, and this nuance
  is a strong live-Q&A answer. NCCN population exclusions (multiple myeloma, acute
  leukemia, myeloproliferative neoplasms, primary/metastatic brain tumors) are
  handled on a separate "excluded" pathway, not scored against Khorana.

- DDI knowledge-base sourcing (per expert review): the 52-agent per-DOAC table is
  curated from a single named source set - the AHA 2022 Scientific Statement [14],
  Hellfritzsch et al. 2024 [15], and FDA DOAC labeling - which is defensible and
  roughly state-of-the-art; Hellfritzsch remains the most granular per-agent/per-DOAC
  reference available and has not been superseded. This module carries the most
  clinical uncertainty (the DOAC-antineoplastic evidence base is thin and CDS
  sources disagree - e.g., Nowinski & Chaireti 2025 found ~35% of pairs flagged by
  >=1 source), so its defense is transparency about that single named source set and
  the 16 individually-anchored major cells, NOT a claim of independent validation.
  Describe the DDI output as "faithful to a single named source set," never as
  "accurate" unqualified. The EHRA Practical Guide is the most widely-used clinical
  DOAC-interaction reference and is a reasonable pointer for clinicians, but it is
  NOT a curation input to this KB and is not claimed as a source. The switch-to-LMWH
  logic when a strong DDI precludes a DOAC follows ITAC 2022 (grade 1A) [8] and ACC
  2026 [13].

- Guideline currency (per expert review): the encoded primary-prophylaxis rules are
  current as of NCCN v1.2026 [7], ITAC 2022 [8], ASCO 2023 [12], and ACC 2026 [13],
  and nothing published 2024-2026 changes them (threshold >=2, apixaban/rivaroxaban
  preferred, LMWH fallback all stand). Out-of-scope-but-tracked developments: API-CAT
  (NEJM 2025; reduced-dose apixaban 2.5 mg BID noninferior for EXTENDED secondary
  prevention - treatment, not primary prophylaxis), TARGET-TP (biomarker-guided
  prophylaxis in lung/GI; reinforces the lung Khorana caveat), and factor XI
  inhibitors (abelacimab: ASTER/MAGNOLIA) as the emerging CAT anticoagulation class.

- Renal-cell carcinoma (per expert review): C64 is deliberately NOT scored (0 pts),
  matching NCCN, which scores only bladder and testicular among GU cancers. RCC risk
  is nonetheless genuinely elevated - contemporary US data show ~7.6% 12-month VTE
  incidence, above bladder (~4.8%) and testicular (~3.8%) [3] - so the engine
  surfaces it as an advisory + a bleeding-risk factor rather than letting a contested
  rule silently cross the >=2 threshold. This "score the guideline, flag the gap"
  discipline is a feature to foreground, not a limitation to hide.
